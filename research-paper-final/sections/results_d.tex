\subsection{A learned regulatory term opens an information gap of its own}
\label{sec:res-hybrid}

A neural--mechanistic hybrid replaces one closed-form regulation with a small
network and keeps the rest of the model; for the \bcat{}$\to$MYC induction,
$\eta_{BM}H_2(b;\kappa_{BM})$ becomes a network $\fnn(b)$ beside the basal
production rate $a_M$ (Eq~\eqref{eq:myc-hybrid}). Run through the full PINN
pipeline under the ten base conditions, the hybrid learns the function well
(normalised RMSE 6--10\% in the four regimes) and loses the basal rate beside
it: $a_M$ is 32.3\% and 52.1\% off in the two low-WNT regimes, where the
mechanistic model recovers it to 0.4\% and 4.6\% (Fig~\ref{fig:learned}A;
run \runid{20260727\_012448\_bm\_myc}). The RA$\to$HOXA5 hybrid loses $a_5$ by
9--37\% (\runid{20260726\_214316\_ra\_h5}). Is this an estimator gap or an
information gap?

The arithmetic suggests the latter. On the observed support, for any constant
$c$,
\begin{equation}
a + \fnn(u) = (a + c) + \big[\fnn(u) - c\big],
\label{eq:compensation}
\end{equation}
and the bracket is a function the network represents as well as $\fnn$
wherever the regulator $u$ is bounded away from zero. A constraint $f(0)=0$ (the
\emph{anchor}) removes the constant from the model at one point; it removes it
from the fit only if the data visit that point. In the base design they do not.
The lowest \bcat{} level across all conditions and regimes is 0.036, an anchor
ratio (Eq~\eqref{eq:ratio}) of 0.025--0.081; in the two high-WNT regimes the
lowest value, 0.20, is the initial condition itself, so what little support
near the anchor exists there is set by the choice of initial state, not by the
dynamics. The lowest RA level is 0.136. In the Early Adenoma fit the learned MYC
activation satisfies $\fnn(0)=0$ exactly, but over the observed range
($b\ge0.11$) it sits a near-constant 0.094 above the true Hill function, and
$a_M$ is low by the same 0.094 ($\hat a_M=0.086$ against 0.18;
Fig~\ref{fig:learned}A). The identity is close to automatic for any fit that
reproduces the production flux with the correct timescale; what is informative
is that the offset is nearly constant across the support. Across the sixteen
equation-local fits of four network parameterisations in four regimes (below)
the mean offset equals the basal deficit to within 0.005
(Fig~\ref{fig:learned}B). In the full pipeline it holds in Early Adenoma and
approximately in Normal (offset 0.067 against a deficit of 0.058), and not in
the two high-WNT regimes; there, and in Normal, the fit also inflates the MYC
timescale $\varepsilon_M$ (0.69--0.72 against 0.60). This is the
constant-offset form of the degeneracy that Loman and Baker construct for a
linear-rate term~\citep{loman2025identifiability}, and it is consistent with an
instance reported in the literature, a learned nuclear-export term recovered
by symbolic regression as $3.96\times10^{-4}x+0.8$ against a true
$6.17\times10^{-2}x$~\citep{philipps2025ude}.

If the gap were an estimator gap, a better-shaped network would close it.
Loman and Baker found that monotone, bounded networks bring parametric
identifiability on a par with the fully known model in a linear-pathway
model~\citep{loman2025identifiability}. To test this at scale we used an
equation-local screen (Methods): only the equation that hosts the learned edge
is fitted, from exact states, by the network and, from the same seeds and
budget, by the mechanistic control. Because the states are exact, the screen
has no measurement noise; its errors are those of the optimisation and of the
model class, and its absolute values bound the full pipeline from below. Across
the seven production edges of the model (S2~Table), a monotone network with an
exact anchor (\texttt{sc}) sometimes halves the basal error of the
non-monotone gated network (\texttt{gated}) and sometimes quadruples it;
averaged over regimes, the gated network leaves the basal parameter 16--50\%
off and the monotone one 14--74\%, against 7--25\% for the mechanistic control
(Fig~\ref{fig:learned}C). In the two low-WNT regimes, where the control
recovers all fourteen basal parameters to below 7.5\%, the two networks recover
4 of 28. In the two high-WNT regimes the control degrades as well (all 14 of
its failures are there), and the comparison is between two failures. For the
MYC edge all five parameterisations were run in all regimes
(Fig~\ref{fig:learned}D): where the control recovers $a_M$ to 0.0--0.1\%, the
gated, monotone and linear-anchor networks leave it 6.5--38\% off, the
monotone-rate form 200--400\% off (it fails through the timescale, which it
inflates to 1.2--18.3), and the bounded form 10.5--17.9\%, although it is handed
the true asymptote; in the two high-WNT regimes the bounded form (8.4\%,
18.5\%) beats a control that has itself degraded (15.6\%, 35.6\%). No network
parameterisation closes the gap where the control is sound. Restart noise is
large away from the anchor: the same MYC-edge cell (Advanced Adenoma, gated)
reads 68.3\% in the two-restart atlas and 18.9\% in the three-restart
five-form run, so cells are compared within an experiment, not across.
Modulator edges, where the network \emph{multiplies} a state ($f(u)\cdot y$)
and there is no additive constant to absorb, behave differently: CYP26A1$\dashv$RA
and HOXA5$\dashv$\bcat{} are learned to 0.0--1.7\% functional error with every
surviving equation parameter recovered in the low-WNT regimes (run
\runid{20260801\_204046\_screen\_atlas}).

\subsection{The Fisher information of a learned term}
\label{sec:res-hfim}

The screen has no measurement noise, so its Fisher information is formally
infinite. To ask the same question of it that Fig~\ref{fig:info} asked of the
mechanistic model, we treat the residual of the hosting equation as noisy with
an arbitrary scale $\sigma_r$ and compute $\Gamma$, the CRLB of the basal parameter
per unit $\sigma_r$, with the learned term represented by the screen's own
network, fitted to the true regulation and profiled out (Methods). $\Gamma$ is then
a measure of how well the design conditions the basal parameter against
everything the network can do; we report it relative to the same quantity for
the mechanistic control, $\Gamma_{\text{net}}/\Gamma_{\text{mech}}$, the information lost
to the network. For a network that is not observed near its anchor the
network block is nearly rank-deficient, and the absolute value of $\Gamma$ depends on
the numerical rank assigned to it, by a factor of 17 to 5000 between relative
tolerances $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-10}$; the size of the base-design loss is
therefore not determined, while its ordering, the design factors where the
anchor is visited, and every rank correlation below (which move by at most 0.04)
are. We quote values at tolerance $10^{-8}$. Against Monte Carlo refits of the MYC edge it is
calibrated where the anchor is visited (empirical and predicted standard
deviation within 3\% for the network and 1\% for an anchored cubic). Where it is
not visited, the bound at tolerance $10^{-8}$ overstates the empirical scatter of
a Levenberg--Marquardt refit 23-fold, and a tolerance of $10^{-5}$ brings it to
within 12\%; the size of the base-design loss quoted below is therefore an
upper estimate, and only orderings and ratios are used in what follows.

Three results (Fig~\ref{fig:hfim}). First, under the base design the network
loses a factor of 56 to $4\times10^5$ in basal-rate precision relative to the
mechanistic control of the same equation, and the loss is a property of the
network's flexibility on the unobserved interval: an anchored cubic, which
cannot bend freely between $u=0$ and $\min u$, is within a factor of four of the
control. Second, two
information-matched conditions change $\Gamma$ by a factor 0.90--0.99 for the
network and the mechanistic control alike (0.64--0.99 across all
representations), whereas a depletion pair that reaches the anchor of the
RA-driven edges shrinks it by a factor of $1.8$--$6.1\times10^{-4}$ and brings the network
to 1.0--1.4 times its control (Fig~\ref{fig:hfim}A); for the mechanistic
control the same pair helps three- to twentyfold. Depletion does not add general
information, it removes the loss that the network introduced. For the
\bcat{}$\to$MYC edge, the double knockdown that takes \bcat{} to zero brings the
network to its control in every regime, while the single WNT knockdown, which
leaves \bcat{} at 1.3\% of its range, leaves a loss of 65--210. Within each dose
arm, $\Gamma$ falls monotonically with the achieved anchor ratio (Spearman $\ge0.99$
in all six arm--regime series, Fig~\ref{fig:hfim}B), but the arms do not
collapse onto one curve: at matched $\rho\approx0.016$ in Severe APC Loss the
loss is 3.5 for the retinoid arm and 230 for the \bcat{} arm. The anchor ratio
orders designs within an arm; the bound weighs them across arms.

Third, the bound is consistent with the fits, which were all run before it was
computed. Over 116 distinct screen fits (atlas, depletion and
information-matched comparisons, dose-response, prospective test), the rank
correlation between $\Gamma$ and the measured basal error is $+0.79$, against $+0.55$
for the anchor ratio alone (Fig~\ref{fig:hfim}C). The correlation is carried by
design changes: within the base design, where edges differ in equations rather
than in what the data visit, it is $+0.32$ ($n=56$); and the mechanistic $\Gamma$
correlates with the controls' errors about as well ($+0.80$), so $\Gamma$ measures
the conditioning of the equation as much as the network's loss. The cells share
edges and regimes, so we give no $p$-value. One discordance is instructive.
Under the single-knockdown depletion pair the bound predicts a similar
residual loss for the \bcat{}$\to$MYC and \bcat{}$\to$CYP26A1 edges (65--210 and
23--142), yet the fit improved the former thirty-fold and the latter not at all
(below): the bound ranks designs, not individual fits.

Two edges show what the bound adds to the anchor ratio. For MYC$\to$HOXA13,
neither the depletion pairs nor a \bcat{} double knockout lowers the loss below
$2.6\times10^3$ in any regime, although the latter cuts the MYC level; only a
protocol that also silences basal MYC transcription (\texttt{mycKO}: the
\bcat{} double knockout plus $k_{aM}=0$) brings it to 1.01 in all four regimes.
For HOXA13$\to$\bcat{}, whose basal parameter is $W$ itself, every protocol
that drives HOXA13 to zero leaves the loss unchanged ($6.5\times10^4$ to
$7.7\times10^5$): HOXA13 can only be silenced by first removing \bcat{}, whose
production is $k_WW+k_{13}\eta_{13}H_2(h_{13})$, so the conditions that visit
the anchor set $k_W=0$ and delete $W$ from their own equations. The bound is
consistent with the second clause of the design rule that the prospective test
below arrived at empirically. It also points to a remedy the rule missed: the
\emph{partial} WNT knockdown $k_W=0.02$, which does not reach the anchor
($\rho=0.16$), brings the loss for $W$ to 1.6--2.0, because two conditions that
observe $W$ and $0.02\,W$ with a known knockdown efficiency identify $W$ with no
anchor at all.

\subsection{Designs that visit the anchor restore the basal rate}
\label{sec:res-dose}

The fits that the bound summarises are, in order of stringency, as follows.
Adding the depletion pair (the WNT knockdown $k_W=0.02$ and a retinoid-free
medium, which drives RA to zero exactly) to the ten base conditions takes the
basal error of the two RA-driven edges from 10.9--33.5\% to 0.09--1.1\%, and
the RA$\to$HOXA5 edge in Severe APC Loss from no equation parameter recovered
to all four (Fig~\ref{fig:depletion}A). The MYC edge, whose regulator only
approaches the anchor, improves up to thirty-fold but stops at 2.5--2.9\%; the
\bcat{}$\to$CYP26A1 edge does not improve (24.6\%$\to$25.5\% in Normal,
30.0\%$\to$64.8\% in Severe). Adding instead the information-matched pair, two
conditions of the same size and style that leave every anchor ratio unchanged,
changes the basal error by a factor of 0.76--1.54 in both directions and the
equation parameters recovered from 16 to 15 of 28, whereas the depletion pair
cuts it 7- to 200-fold on three of the four edges (not on
\bcat{}$\to$CYP26A1) and recovers 26 of 28 (Fig~\ref{fig:depletion}B). These
two comparisons used one restart, and away from the anchor single cells move by
tens of points between restarts; the replicated contrast is the next one.

\paragraph{At fixed experimental effort} Depletion and information-matched
conditions differ in what they reach and also in how strongly they perturb. A
dose-response with the number of conditions fixed separates the two
(Fig~\ref{fig:dose}A,B). At every dose the design is the ten base conditions
plus one graded depletion of depth $k\in\{1,0.3,0.1,0.03,0.01,0\}$, so no dose
has more data than another and $k=1$ is each arm's own null. The three arms are
graded retinoid restriction (scoring $a_5$); a graded WNT knockdown alone
(scoring $a_M$), under which \bcat{} plateaus at 1.3\% of its range because the
HOXA13 feedback keeps producing it; and the same knockdown applied to both
sources of \bcat{} ($k_W=k_{13}=k$), which reaches zero. The last two score the
same edge, equation and parameter with the same seeds, budget and number of
conditions, and at $k=0$ differ by one knockdown. Replicated with five restarts
from three disjoint seed sets, the single knockdown leaves $a_M$ at 1.45\%
(range 1.33--1.52) in Normal and 2.25\% (1.79--2.63) in Severe APC Loss; the
double knockdown at 0.003\% (0.001--0.008) and 0.002\% (0.001--0.003), 500- and
1100-fold lower, against a seed spread of 0.2 and 0.8 points. The single
knockdown at $k=0$ removes the WNT drive completely, and the mechanistic control
is then at 0.0\%; \bcat{}, still produced through the HOXA13 feedback, stops at
1.3\% of its range, and the network's basal error stops with it. Against dose the arms separate. Against the
achieved anchor ratio the two \bcat{} arms line up (at matched ratio they agree
within a factor of about two) and the single-knockdown arm sits at its ratio
floor, but the retinoid arm does not join them in Severe APC Loss: there it
stays near 3\% down to ratio zero, at parity with its mechanistic control (3.4\%),
whose own information about $a_5$ is limited in that regime. The anchor ratio
orders doses within an arm; how much a given ratio is worth differs between
edges, as the Fisher analysis predicted (Fig~\ref{fig:hfim}B). From its own null
dose, the MYC edge in Severe APC Loss goes from 61\% to 0.001\%. The monotone
exact-anchor network behaves the same way (retinoid arm 22.8\%$\to$0.0\% in
Normal; double knockdown 70.3\%$\to$0.0\% in Severe; single knockdown stops at
1.8\% and 2.4\%).

Six predictions for this experiment were registered before it was run (S1~Text).
As registered, with two restarts in Severe APC Loss, two of the six passed in
both regimes; rerunning Severe with five restarts, which removed one bad
optimum, three pass. The failures include the registered falsifier in Normal:
we had predicted that the single knockdown would never fall below 2\%, and it
reached 1.3\%; at matched anchor ratio the two \bcat{} arms agree to within a
factor of 2.0 (Normal) and 2.3 (Severe), which is what the anchor account
predicts, while at ratio zero the double knockdown is three orders of magnitude
lower.

With state estimation in the loop, the full pipeline confirms the contrast. With
the double knockdown and the retinoid-free medium added, the MYC basal error
falls from 10.3/11.7/17.4/16.4\% under the single knockdown to
1.4/0.6/0.6/0.7\% across the regimes, against a mechanistic control of
0.2--0.3\% on the same twelve conditions; the hybrid recovers 58 of 136 shared
regime--parameter pairs against the control's 62, three of the four lost pairs
in the MYC equation (S3~Table). One further knockdown turns a 10--17\% error
into one within about a point of the fully mechanistic fit; the full-pipeline errors
are larger than the screen's, as expected from state-estimation error.

\paragraph{A prospective test} Because the anchor ratio needs only reference
trajectories, it can be tabulated for a ladder of candidate protocols ordered by
laboratory cost before any fit (S4~Table), naming for each learned edge the
cheapest protocol that puts its anchor in the data. The table was tested on the
two production edges with the largest functional error in the atlas, MYC$\to$HOXA13
and HOXA13$\to$\bcat{}, neither of which had been run under any depletion, each
in three arms: the base design; a \emph{near-miss}, a larger perturbation than
any in the base design that leaves the edge's anchor ratio far from zero; and
the prescribed protocol (Fig~\ref{fig:dose}C). For MYC$\to$HOXA13 the prescribed
protocol (\texttt{mycKO}) took the basal error from 12.8\% to 0.1\% in Normal
and from 95.6\% to 0.05\% in Severe APC Loss, while the near-miss, the \bcat{}
double knockout, which moves the anchor ratio from 0.082 to 0.079 (Normal) and
0.113 to 0.068 (Severe), left it at 51.5\% and 59.8\%, in Normal worse than
doing nothing. For HOXA13$\to$\bcat{} the registered prediction failed: the
basal error did not move under any arm (16.2\% in all three in Normal, 57.5\%
to 54.7\% in Severe). The protocol that reaches its anchor, a HOXA13 knockout
($k_{a13}=0$ on top of \texttt{mycKO}), necessarily includes $k_W=0$ and so
deletes the parameter it was meant to identify. The design rule therefore has
two clauses, \emph{reach the anchor, with a condition that still contains the
basal term}; the second was formulated after this failure, and the Fisher
analysis above is consistent with it. Of the five registered predictions, the two
that carry the claim passed (the prescribed arm below 3\%; the near-miss above
8\%), and three failed (S1~Text). The prescribed protocol also made the
\emph{functional} error of the MYC$\to$HOXA13 network worse (57\%$\to$68\% in
Normal, 12\%$\to$81\% in Severe) while it fixed the parameter: enlarging the
support towards zero exposes the network to inputs on which it was never
constrained, so functional and parametric identifiability moved in opposite
directions, the distinction Loman and Baker draw~\citep{loman2025identifiability}
made concrete.
