# Poster A — abstract

**Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Forward Solution and Parameter Inference in a WNT–RA–HOX Model of Colorectal Cancer Initiation**

Colorectal cancer initiation is driven by loss of APC function and the resulting deregulation of WNT/β-catenin signaling, which retinoic acid (RA) antagonizes through the HOX transcription factors. We present a reduced ordinary differential equation model of the coupled WNT–RA–HOX network with 36 kinetic parameters. Four regimes, from Normal to Severe APC Loss, reproduce the adenoma–carcinoma sequence through increasing WNT drive and decreasing APC functionality. We ask whether physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) can solve this system and invert it. The dynamics are stiff and multiscale, with circadian RA forcing and a slow treatment pulse, and a standard multilayer perceptron fails to resolve them through spectral bias. A multiscale Fourier-feature time embedding removes this failure: the forward PINN reproduces implicit Radau reference trajectories with 1.06% mean relative error under dense supervision and 2.41% from 40 sparse observations, in every regime. In the inverse direction, recovery saturates well below the full parameter set, for two reasons. First, a derivative-free integral residual removes the systematic bias of automatic-differentiation derivatives and raises the number of parameters recovered to within 10% from 37 to 50 of 144. Second, the remaining ceiling of 13–18 of 36 parameters per regime is reproduced by classical ODE fitting on the same data, and is therefore a property of the experiment, not the network. In high-WNT regimes β-catenin saturates and APC functionality becomes practically non-identifiable. Inverse accuracy is thus limited by the information content of the data, not by network expressiveness.
